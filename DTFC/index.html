Ditchling Thursday Football Club is a village (in both meanings of the word) football club based in Ditchling, East Sussex, England. They currently play their home matches at Downlands Astro at 8 pm on Thursday nights, in the shadow of Ditchling Beacon. The club is known for its ambiguity over what constitutes ‘head height’, exactly how teams should be picked (and checked), and just how late you can drop out after saying you’d play.

History

Origins

Ditchling Thursday Football Club has its origins in an indoor six-a-side side that played on Monday evenings, kicking off at 9:00 pm, at the Downlands sports hall in Hassocks, West Sussex. The founding squad – known within the club as the "kitty" – comprised Nick Beacroft, Rob Beacroft, Paul Dales, Mike Hardaker, Hugo Harries, Simon Hickman, Will Hudson, Ed Moore, Sab Petruccelli, Al Rothwell, Smudge Tyler-Smith and Russell Venn-Taylor.[1]

Move outdoors (2020–present)

Following the disruption caused by the COVID-19 pandemic in early 2020, the club reconvened outdoors, moving to Thursday evenings with an 8:00 pm kick-off and adopting the name by which it is now known. The first match under the new format took place on 24 September 2020.[2]

After a number of brief interruptions during late 2020 and early 2021, the club settled into a regular calendar, playing continuously from early September through to late July. It has maintained this pattern in every season since.

Team sizes are famously fluid, dictated by the manager's Monday roll-call and the inevitable Thursday-morning drop-outs. Across the club's 237 recorded outdoor fixtures, 8-a-side (8 v 8) is the most common format at roughly 46% of games, closely followed by 7 v 7 (30%). Balanced numbers are far from guaranteed and on occasion threadbare, with 3 v 4 being the smallest game in the club's history, and one still spoken of in hushed tones.

Rules

A set of playing rules was established by Paul Dales following the first week's trial and error. Five rules were adopted:

  1. No play above head height, unless the ball has come off a goalkeeper's save or the woodwork.
  2. Throw-ins are taken as kick-ins.
  3. Any player may enter the goal area; goalkeepers may come out, but cannot handle the ball outside the area.
  4. No slide tackles.
  5. An honesty policy applies to everything.

The rules have remained largely unchanged ever since, although the true meaning of "head height" has never been firmly established. A back-pass rule was introduced in 2023.

Stats

The club maintained a controversial statistical record for two seasons. The 2023–24 campaign was the domain of self-appointed “stats man” James Clark who, with time freed up by a bout of sciatica, logged appearances, goals, win percentages among others. Man-of-the-match awards and expected-goals (xG) were discussed but not implemented.

For 2024–25 the keys to the spreadsheet passed to Nick Beacroft, who tracked everything from captaincy win–loss records to the much-cited finding that Joe Scannell held a 0% win rate whenever his mum picked the teams. The statistical era peaked across these two seasons before quietly winding down following a vote at the end of the 2024–25 season.

For the 2025–26 season Joe Scannell recorded his own personal stats (including assists), updated (and often vocally shared) in the White Horse post match.

Ground

The club plays its home fixtures on the outdoor all-weather astro pitches at the back of Downlands School in Hassocks. The astro is divided into thirds, and the club has always occupied the furthest pitch, kicking off at 8:00 pm. The surface was adopted in 2020 in preference to the indoor hall for its greater size and “more time on the ball”. A strict footwear policy is enforced (read encouraged): astro boots or trainers only, with studs, moulded soles and blades ‘banned’.

Despite the “all-weather” billing, the pitch is famously susceptible to the elements. Fixtures have been lost to frost, snow and flooding.

The Big Match

Once a year, Ditchling Thursday FC abandon the familiarity of a third of a pitch and take their footballing philosophy to the grandest stage; a full 11-a-side fixture against unknown opposition. It is the one occasion on which they play with big goals, a proper ball, throw-ins, the offside rule and a referee.

As a squad of middle-aged men set out once more to prove to themselves – reputations are forged, hamstrings are tested.

The Big Match: Cambridge Touring Team

Ditchling Thursday FC 3 – Cambridge Touring Team 1
Saturday 20 April 2024, 12.15 pm kick-off, Stanley Deason Leisure Centre, Brighton

Match report: Fired up by pre-match words of wisdom from none other than Paul Merson, the Tangerine Thunderbastards announced themselves to the wider footballing world in their maiden 11-a-side “Big Game” with a statement victory, executing gaffer Paul “Klopp” Dales’ four-quarter masterplan to perfection. Gareth James, Will Hudson and a marauding Rob Beacroft all beat an opposition goalkeeper reliably reported to be some 70 years of age – a famous triumph of youth over experience – the only blot being the freak felling of the opposition’s “Guffy”, collateral damage in a display of such physical authority (that Rob Beacroft was repeatedly asked to tone down).

Matchday squad: Jeremy Allan, James Ardagh, Nick Beacroft, Rob Beacroft, Ron Chiwome, James Clark, James CP, Paul Dales, Jamie Fries, Gareth James, Hugo Harries, Will Hudson, Andy McDowell, Ed Moore, Sabino Petruccelli, Neil Phillips, Henry Smith, Smudge, Russell Venn-Taylor.
Scorers: Gareth James, Will Hudson and Rob Beacroft.

The Big Match II: Middle Pitch

Ditchling Thursday FC 4 – Middle Pitch FC 5
Saturday 21 June 2025, 2.05 pm kick-off, The Burgess Hill Academy (4G astro)

Match report: The Big Game II will forever be remembered as the day the Ditch were robbed. Facing opponents who had – in flagrant breach of the spirit of the fixture – fielded a clutch of fresh-legged twenty-somethings, and hauling themselves back from 5–1 down through a Ron “Ronaldo” Chiwome hat-trick and a James Ardagh screamer, Simon’s side were denied a stone-cold Will Hudson penalty that would surely have levelled it. The lone blemish on the day was the loss of Hugo Harries, who came off injured with a broken wrist – proof, if any were needed, that the Big Match takes no prisoners.

Matchday squad: Jeremy Allan, James Ardagh, Ron Chiwome, James Clark, James CP, Paul Dales, Hugh Felton, Jamie Fries, Hugo Harries, Simon Hickman, Charley Higginbotham, Will Hudson, Mike Linsey, Ed Moore, Gavin Richmond, Henry Smith, Smudge, David Sumnall.
Scorers: Ron Chiwome (3) and James Ardagh.

The Big Match III: Hassocks Dads

Ditchling Thursday FC 3 – Hassocks Dads 3
Friday 26 June 2026, 6.15 pm kick-off, Downlands Astro

Match report: Under an amber heat-health warning The Big Match III served up the finest instalment of the trilogy – a breathless 3–3 draw. Ron Chiwome, Will Hudson and Charlie Draper all found the net as the orange army knocked it about on the deck, before the Hassocks dads hauled level in a sun-baked finale which also saw the surprise, and entirely unsanctioned, fourth-quarter ‘pitch invasion’ of Ed Moore. As organiser Joe (who defied six weeks of groin trouble to take the field) put it: “it may have been a draw, but football was the winner.”

Matchday squad: James Ardagh, Nick Beacroft, Charlie Draper, Ron Chiwome, Rob Corbett, James CP, Paul Dales, Duncan, Hugh Felton, Jamie Fries, Hugo Harries, Simon Hickman, Will Hudson, Andy McDowell, Sabino Petruccelli, Joe Scannell, Henry Smith.
Scorers: Ron Chiwome, Will Hudson and Charlie Draper.
